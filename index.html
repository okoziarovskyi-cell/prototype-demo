RISORA / FOUNDING PM TEST1
In short: the three calls I'd make

If you read one slide

Wedge

Pattern-Breakers, 28-45, women-skewed

People with one named recurring pattern (people-pleasing, conflict avoidance, overthinking) who already took a personality test. The simulation is a rehearsal of the exact conversation they keep losing. This buyer pays the most and the change shows up in weeks.

Session

One real scene, one hard choice, one visible consequence

A great session gets a "that's me" in the first minute and ends with a task the user does today. Measure change with a next-day real-life check-in, not with completion.

Funnel

Reveal the type, speak one framework, fix trust leaks

The funnel promises "your archetype", never shows it, and mixes four personality frameworks. Fix that and the compliance leaks before buying more traffic.

How I think about the stage: at 100-1,000 paid subs the constraint isn't more features. It's knowing who converts and retains, and making the first week clearly useful for them. Everything below follows from that.

TASK 01 / RESEARCH3
Task 01 / A. Audience and the wedge

Three segments Risora could fit, and the one I'd bet on

Segment A

The Stuck Striver

Who: 25-34, ambitious, feels behind peers, has taken 16Personalities.

Trigger: missed promotion, harsh review, a friend's LinkedIn win.

Sticks if: Career sphere shows visible progress and scenes mirror real work talks.

Risk: outcome is diffuse and slow to prove, competes with Headway and coaching.

Segment B, my pick

The Pattern-Breaker

Who: 28-45, women-skewed, in a relationship rut or burned by the same dynamic at work. Therapy-curious, price and wait-list sensitive.

Trigger: the same fight for the third time, "why do I always say yes", a therapist mentions attachment style.

Sticks if: the chat sim recreates her conversation and gives an off-ramp she can use tonight. Your paywall testimonials already say this.

Segment C

The Type Enthusiast

Who: 18-27, identity play, MBTI memes, Boo/PDB.

Trigger: a share from a friend, TikTok, a new avatar to collect.

Sticks if: avatar evolution and social comparison. A game, not a program.

Risk: lowest willingness to pay, highest churn. Great for virality later, bad first bet.

Why B for the next 6 months

  • The mechanic fits the problem. Chat simulation is rehearsal. Rehearsal changes an interpersonal pattern, it does little for "career growth" in the abstract.
  • Money. Wellness and relationship intents carry the highest willingness to pay in quiz-funnel subs (Finch, Liven, Breeze are all women-skewed 25-45).
  • Proof in weeks. "I said no once this week" is a real, self-reportable outcome. Retention story and marketing story become the same story.

What changes in the product

  • Onboarding asks for the pattern and the person ("with whom does it happen most?"). That becomes the scene generator.
  • First 7 days are one pattern track (People-Pleaser x Partner), not a rotation across 4 spheres.
  • Per-user success metric: a real-life "I did it differently" report within 14 days.

Trade-off: narrower top of funnel for 2 quarters, in exchange for a retention signal we can read with ~500 subs.

TASK 01 / AUDIENCE4
Task 01 / A. What I'd validate before betting

Three questions, each with a cheap test

01

Does a session change behaviour outside the app?

Test: a one-tap next-day check-in ("Did this come up? Did you try the new move?"). Target: at least 30% of D1-active users report a real attempt by D14. Under 10% means the thesis is entertainment, not change.

02

Which pattern retains?

Test: tag every payer by their funnel "what holds you back" answer, read D7 and D30 by tag on the existing ~500 subs. Enough for a rank order, not for precise rates. Build the top 2 tracks deep first.

03

Is the buyer the same person as the retainer?

Test: compare funnel answers of purchasers vs. D30-retained. Split-test the hook, "Discover your archetype" vs. "Find the pattern that holds you back", on the same paid traffic. Read CPA and D7 together, not CPA alone.

Decision rule: if Q1 is at least 30% and Q2 shows a clear winner, we go all-in on that track. If Q1 fails, we stop building content and fix the session before spending on traffic.

How I define and size it (my estimate)

Definition: mobile subscriptions selling a small daily practice that changes how you act: habit apps, micro-CBT, coaching-lite, gamified companions. Personality tests are the cheapest intent signal into it.

TAM 200-400M unique test-takers a year. SAM Tier-1 English mobile users who pay for self-improvement, ~30-50M. SOM in 12-18 months with the quiz-funnel playbook: 50-100k active subs, $10-25M ARR at ~€20 blended. A range, not a forecast. It depends on funnel CPA and D30.

Where it's going

  • LLM role-play is free. ChatGPT will rehearse any conversation. The defensible layer is curriculum, progression and memory of your type, not "AI chat".
  • Test to practice bundling. Liven, Breeze, Sintelly already sell a program behind a quiz. The traffic auction is crowded, so the difference has to be visible inside the funnel.
  • Companions retain. Finch shows that a creature you don't want to let down beats a streak counter. Risora's animal types are an underused asset.

TASK 01 / COMPETITORS7
Task 02 / Daily simulation, my design

My type: INFJ-A, "Advocate"

16personalities free test: 52% Introverted, 73% Intuitive, 53% Feeling, 63% Judging, 63% Assertive. The report flags "sensitive to negative feedback", "struggles to delegate", "overlooks small wins". Together they make one habit I recognise right away.

The pattern this session targets

The 11 pm silent rewrite. A teammate sends work that is 70% there. Instead of handing it back with a clear ask, I quietly redo it late at night. The fix is "faster", the conversation might land as criticism, 70% doesn't clear my bar. The team learns nothing and next week the draft is 70% again.

Behaviour shift being trained

Hand it back with one specific ask, and accept 85% on low-stakes work. When I catch myself opening someone's file to "just fix it", I send a three-line message instead: what's good, the one thing that must change, by when. Measured as hand-backs per week vs. silent rewrites.

Open the clickable prototype Built in the app's design: cards, colours, tab bar, monsters. About 6 minutes of real user time.

The session, minute by minute

0:00
Hook (20 s). Owl companion, dark office: "It's 22:40. Nazar's deck just landed. It's... fine."
0:20
Chat simulation (about 3 min). Three beats with the teammate and my inner voice. Each choice shows the other person's reaction and a "next Tuesday" fallout before any score.
3:20
Mini-game 1, "Three lines" (80 s). Build the hand-back from fragments under a timer. Softeners cost time.
4:40
Mini-game 2, "Good enough" (70 s). Six cards: ship / hand back / fix it yourself, given quality vs. stakes. Fixing costs energy.
5:50
Close (30 s). One-sentence principle, transfer task for today, check-in tomorrow at 09:00, owl gains a feather.

TASK 02 / MY TYPE AND SESSION8
Task 02 / Inside the session

Scene: 22:40, the deck is 70% there, the client call is at 10:00

Beat 1 of 3
Nazar, 22:38: "Sent the client deck! Let me know if anything's off, heading to bed"
Inner voice: slide 4 argues the wrong thing, slide 7 has old numbers. 40 minutes for me, an hour for him and I'd still redo it...
Open the file, fix 4 and 7, send at 00:15: "made a few small tweaks"
"Strong structure. Two things must change before 10:00: slide 4 should argue X, slide 7 needs July numbers. Can you turn it by 9:00?"
Leave it, fix it live on the call
Consequence of option 1: next Tuesday the deck lands at 22:40 again. "You always polish it anyway." You're the bottleneck.
Beat 2: "which matters more, 4 or 7?" trains prioritising out loud. Beat 3: the deck is back at 85%, ship it or rewrite "just the headline".

Why the beats are shaped like this

  • Beat 1 makes the habitual move the most attractive option and lets me take it. The consequence is social, not a red X. That's the "that's me" moment.
  • Beat 2 is the real skill: prioritising out loud. INFJ instinct is "both, sorry". The target answer models a trade-off sentence with a fallback I own.
  • Beat 3 targets "overlooking small wins": accept 85% and say what got better.
  • Debrief names the pattern in one line and gives one sentence to keep. Optional AI chat to rehearse my own version.
Mini-game 1

"Three lines"

Nine fragments float up, tap exactly three: what's good, the one must-change, by when. "Sorry to bother", "no worries if not", "a few things" are traps that cost time. Three rounds, rising stakes.

Mini-game 2

"Good enough"

Six cards with quality (70/85/95%) and stakes (internal draft to board deck). Ship, hand back or fix it yourself. Fixing drains the energy bar. End card: my curve vs. the INFJ default, "you over-fix at medium stakes".

Close

"Hand it back with one ask. Save the rewrite for board-level stakes." Name one thing you'd polish today and send the three lines instead. Tomorrow 09:00: one-tap check-in. That is the number the session is judged on.

TASK 02 / SESSION9
Task 02 / Two short answers

What makes a great Risora session different from a mediocre one?

  • Recognition before instruction. Great: the scene mirrors my week so precisely it's uncomfortable. Mediocre: "a colleague criticises you" with an obvious right answer.
  • A real dilemma. Great: every option costs something and one is what I always do. Mediocre: one hero option, two strawmen. Users game it in a day.
  • Consequence, then coaching. Great: I feel the other person's reaction and see next-week fallout, then get the principle. Mediocre: a green tick and +30 XP.
  • A sentence I can reuse tonight, not "be more assertive".
  • Ends in the world. A transfer task and a next-day check-in, not a streak notification.

How would I measure whether a session changed behaviour?

Not completion, XP or streak. Those measure the game.

  • Transfer rate: share of sessions whose next-day check-in is "tried it". The quality metric per scene.
  • Repeat-scenario delta: serve the same pattern in a new setting 5-7 days later, unannounced. Does the first choice move from the habitual to the target option?
  • Hesitation: time-to-choose on the habitual option should rise. A cheap in-app proxy for "catching yourself".
  • Weekly self-report on a 0-4 scale vs. the onboarding baseline.
  • By type: a scene that moves ENTPs and not ISFJs is a content bug, not a user bug.

FUNNEL / MAP11
Funnel / What leaks and what I'd ship

Four things to fix before buying more traffic

P0 trust

Four personality frameworks in one funnel

Jungian archetypes on "get ready" (Rebel, Innocent, Sage), then 7 "layers", then MBTI bars, then a DISC "Dominance: High" on the paywall. The app delivers 16 MBTI animal types. Promise one thing, deliver another: refunds and store-review risk.

P0 conversion

The result is never revealed, and the teaser isn't personal

No type, no animal before payment. The teaser bars are hard-coded: 57% Introverted, 77% Thinking, 68% Assertive, 51% Prospecting, identical to the ENTJ example in the internal deck, "Identity" listed twice, "Energy" missing.

P1 compliance

Social proof and legal inconsistencies

"673,587 loved" vs "1,620,000+ discovered" vs "600,000+ community". 4.8 stars on both stores at ~1k subs. Consent pre-ticked, refund link points to the privacy policy, renewal copy says "every 4 weeks at €39.98" on 12 and 24-week plans, the wheel "wins" the 50% already applied.

P1 differentiation

The core mechanic is only described

Nine intent questions collected and barely used. The one thing no competitor has, a scene with a choice and a consequence, is a bullet point on the paywall.
