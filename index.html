TASK 01 / RESEARCH2
Task 01 / A. Audience and the wedge

3 segments Risora could fit, and the one I'd bet on

Segment A

Career Climber

Who: 25-34, ambitious, feels behind peers, has taken 16Personalities.

Trigger: missed promotion, harsh review.

Brings: fear of wasting years, wants to "unlock potential".

Sticks if: Career sphere shows visible progress.

Risk: slow to prove, competes with Headway and coaching.

Segment B, my pick

Pattern-Breaker

Who: 28-45, women-skewed, stuck in the same dynamic with a partner or at work. Therapy-curious, price sensitive.

Trigger: the same fight for the 3rd time, "why do I always say yes".

Brings: shame plus hope, and a named pattern (people-pleasing, conflict avoidance, overthinking).

Sticks if: the chat sim recreates her conversation and gives a line she can use tonight.

Segment C

Type Enthusiast

Who: 18-27, MBTI memes, Boo/PDB.

Trigger: a share from a friend, TikTok, a new avatar.

Brings: curiosity and belonging, not pain.

Sticks if: avatar evolution and social comparison.

Risk: lowest willingness to pay, highest churn. Virality later, not the first bet.

Why B for the next 6 months

  • The mechanic fits the problem. A chat simulation is rehearsal, and rehearsal is what changes an interpersonal pattern.
  • Money. Relationship and wellness intents carry the highest willingness to pay in quiz-funnel subs (Finch, Liven, Breeze are all women-skewed 25-45).
  • Proof in weeks. "I said no once this week" is a real, self-reportable outcome.
  • Why not A or C: Climbers buy outcomes we can't show fast and already have ChatGPT and a mentor. Enthusiasts are the growth engine later, not the revenue engine now.

What changes in the product

  • Onboarding asks for the pattern and the person. That becomes the scene generator.
  • First 7 days are 1 pattern track, not a rotation across 4 spheres.
  • Per-user success metric: a real-life "I did it differently" report within 14 days.

Trade-off: narrower top of funnel for 2 quarters, in exchange for a retention signal we can read with ~500 subs.

TASK 01 / AUDIENCE3
Task 01 / A. What I'd validate before betting

3 questions, and how to answer them before touching live users

At 100-1,000 subs I would not run in-app experiments that can break the experience. First: data we already have, then qualitative research, then small tests.

01

Does a session change behaviour outside the app?

How: 8-10 interviews with current payers, 2 weeks after they started: "tell me about the last time a scene came up in real life, what did you do?" Diary study with 5 users for 7 days. Read support tickets and reviews for "I actually did it" stories.

Signal: users can name a concrete real-life moment without prompting.

02

Which pattern retains?

How: existing data first. Join funnel answers ("what holds you back") to D7 and D30 of current payers. ~500 subs gives a rank order, not precise rates. Then interviews with the top 2 patterns to understand why.

Signal: a clear leader across both retention and interview depth.

03

Is the buyer the same person as the retainer?

How: compare funnel answers of purchasers vs. D30-retained. Then a small landing-page test on paid traffic: "Discover your archetype" vs. "Find the pattern that holds you back". Only the hook changes, the funnel does not, so nothing breaks in the app.

Signal: CPA and D7 read together, not CPA alone.

Decision rule: if interviews and data point to the same 1-2 patterns, we build those tracks deep. If users can't name a real-life moment, we fix the session before spending on traffic.

How I define and size it (my estimate)

Definition: mobile subscriptions selling a small daily practice that changes how you act: habit apps, micro-CBT, coaching-lite, gamified companions. Personality tests are the cheapest intent signal into it.

TAM 200-400M unique test-takers a year. SAM Tier-1 English mobile users who pay for self-improvement, ~30-50M. SOM in 12-18 months with the quiz-funnel playbook: 50-100k active subs, $10-25M ARR at ~€20 blended. A range, not a forecast. It depends on funnel CPA and D30.

Where it's going

  • LLM role-play is free. ChatGPT will rehearse any conversation. The defensible layer is curriculum, progression and memory of your type, not "AI chat".
  • Test to practice bundling. Liven, Breeze, Sintelly already sell a program behind a quiz. The traffic auction is crowded, so the difference has to be visible inside the funnel.
  • Companions retain. Finch shows that a creature you don't want to let down beats a streak counter. Risora's animal types are an underused asset.

Sources: JobCannon report 2026, Sparrow Apps on Finch, Forbes on Headway, SKELAR. All rounded.

TASK 01 / COMPETITORS6
Task 02 / Daily simulation, my design

My type: INFJ-A, "Advocate"

16personalities free test: 52% Introverted, 73% Intuitive, 53% Feeling, 63% Judging, 63% Assertive. The report flags "sensitive to negative feedback", "struggles to delegate", "overlooks small wins". Together they make one habit I recognise right away.

The pattern this session targets

The 11 pm silent rewrite. A teammate sends work that is 70% there. Instead of handing it back with a clear ask, I quietly redo it late at night. The fix is "faster", the conversation might land as criticism, 70% doesn't clear my bar. The team learns nothing and next week the draft is 70% again.

Behaviour shift being trained

Hand it back with one specific ask, and accept 85% on low-stakes work. When I catch myself opening someone's file to "just fix it", I send a 3-line message instead: what's good, the one thing that must change, by when. Measured as hand-backs per week vs. silent rewrites.

Mini-game 1, "3 lines" (80 s). Build the hand-back from fragments under a timer. Softeners cost time.

Mini-game 2, "Good enough" (70 s). 6 cards: ship / hand back / fix it yourself, given quality vs. stakes. Fixing costs energy.

Close (30 s). One-sentence principle, transfer task for today, check-in tomorrow at 09:00, owl gains a feather.
Mini-game 1

"3 lines"

9 fragments, tap exactly 3: what's good, the one must-change, by when. Softeners like "sorry to bother" are traps that cost time. 3 rounds, rising stakes.

Mini-game 2

"Good enough"

6 cards with quality (70/85/95%) and stakes. Ship, hand back or fix it yourself. Fixing drains the energy bar. End card: my curve vs. the INFJ default.

TASK 02 / MY TYPE AND SESSION7
Task 02 / 2 short answers

What makes a great Risora session different from a mediocre one?

  • The scene mirrors my week so precisely it's uncomfortable, not "a colleague criticises you".
  • Every option costs something and one of them is what I always do. No hero option, no strawmen.
  • I see the consequence first, the principle second. Then 1 sentence I can say tonight.
  • It ends with a task in the real world and a check-in tomorrow, not a streak notification.

How would I measure whether a session changed behaviour?

  • Transfer rate: share of sessions where the next-day check-in says "tried it".
  • Repeat-scenario delta: same pattern, new setting, 5-7 days later, unannounced. Does the first choice move?
  • Hesitation: time-to-choose on the habitual option should grow.
  • Weekly self-report on a 0-4 scale vs. onboarding baseline, split by type.

What works: pacing

5-question blocks with a reveal in between. The inkblot "results" are small payoffs that actually feel good. Auto-advance keeps momentum.

What works: curiosity build-up

Layer wheel, "superpower locked", blurred bars, iceberg. It builds well and lands on a paywall that lists the product, not just "the report".

What works: commerce

Per-day price anchor, "recommended for your profile", Apple and Google Pay first, FAQ that answers "where do I get it". Standard, solid.

FUNNEL / MAP9
Funnel / Summary of what I'd fix or test, in priority order

What works, keep it

  • Progress bar on top and 1-tap Likert with auto-advance. Best practice, nothing to add.
  • Inkblot "results": low-friction value inside the test.
  • "Your potential is High" screen: good encouragement before the ask.
  • Age, satisfaction, daily goal: one-click, clean.
  • 65% exit offer for high-friction users on the paywall. Good.

Bugs and polish

  • PayPal is listed on the paywall but not available at checkout.
  • Spheres: I picked 2 with no ranking, later the funnel shows Career as my "#1 focus". Wrong data shown back to the user.
  • Teaser bars are hard-coded (57% Introverted, 77% Thinking...), "Identity" twice, "Energy" missing.
  • No back button in the web funnel, only swipe or browser back. Applies to every screen.
  • Lock icons on the profile screen are dead taps. 3D cube looks cheap next to the rest.
  • Spacing and alignment on "get ready", tree result and the profile screen.
